# The Words That Move

### A compiled glossary of twenty-three parts and eighteen repositories, and what the survey turns up

*Ismaël Joffroy Chandoutis*

*July 2026*

---

This part is a glossary, and it is also something else. Compiling the vocabulary used across twenty-three parts and eighteen public repositories written between 2024 and 2026 yields the expected inventory: coined terms, theoretical borrowings, technical vocabulary. But it also yields, unsought, an audit.

A corpus written alongside a practice is never reread as a book. Notions settle into it, drift, get forgotten. Some are announced in method descriptions and never appear in the texts. Others change meaning between two parts written a month apart, without the contradiction ever being posed. An alphabetical survey makes those movements visible for the first time.

## What this dossier contains

Four files, organised by kind of term rather than by part of origin.

**[Coined concepts](concepts-forges.md)** — notions produced or redefined within the corpus: *écriture liquide*, *non-captivity*, *the tap and the loop*, *the persona factory*, *the memo with no author*, *traboulage*. With, at the end, the list of expected and absent terms.

**[Theoretical borrowings](emprunts-theoriques.md)** — concepts taken from others and whether the usage departs from the canonical one: Farocki's *operative images*, Varoufakis's *technofeudalism*, Chatonsky's *vectofascism* after Wark, the *Kuleshov effect*, Lacan's *big Other*.

**[Technical vocabulary](vocabulaire-technique.md)** — eighty entries, each flagged as standard industry usage or a departure from it: *contracted / connected / active*, *open weights*, *memory bandwidth*, *latent*, *guardrail*, *distillation*.

**[Public repositories](depots-publics.md)** — the vocabulary specific to the eighteen repositories outside this series, whose titles are themselves theses: *language does not exist*, *the right format does not exist*, *don't break the machine*, *own your hands*. And **latencer**, the most strongly coined concept outside this series.

## The five notions that carry the corpus

**Écriture liquide** is the only one spanning the whole period, 2024 to 2026, and every register. It is also the one whose extension widens most: a voluntary discipline at first, it becomes an infrastructure requirement (part 06), then a property of the tool itself (part 10, "liquid in both senses").

**Non-captivity** appears only once, in part 16, and yet supplies the operational definition of sovereignty for the entire repository: being able to change tools without losing what you have built, distinguishing a market from a fief. A central notion used exactly once is an anomaly worth recording.

**The tap and the loop** (part 16) fits in a sentence — "the loop is yours, the tap is his" — accompanied by a reason that is not doctrinal but practical: aligned models refuse to write the material these films are made of.

**The persona factory** (part 08) organises the mirror between a filmmaker building hundreds of agents and a filmed subject who built hundreds of accounts, with its rule: "I only clone the dead."

**The memo with no author** (part 18) defines what "circulates with the formal authority of expertise and zero attached responsibility." It is the exact negative of the authorship note published in part 15 — one describes what to avoid, the other commits to the opposite.

## The displacements

This is the part of the survey that was not planned, and the most useful.

**Sovereignty** begins by designating hardware purchase (part 02), becomes a hygiene of memory (06), then a matter of interoperability (16), then non-alignment between geopolitical blocs (17), then a layer-by-layer count — capital, silicon, weights (22). The word holds; what changes each time is the scale of the adversary. This is not incoherence but progressive extension — except that it is never made explicit.

**Tap**, by contrast, undergoes a clean reversal. In part 16 it is the substrate nothing escapes. In part 17, written a month later, it is the closed API that open weights precisely do escape — and the plural appears, "every tap, American or Chinese." Two parts, one month apart, opposite conclusions about the same object.

**Distillation** produces the corpus's sharpest contradiction. Part 21 treats it as an unprovable IP-theft accusation, stressing that one cannot prove a model was *not* distilled. Part 08 claims the word for a creative gesture: "a distillation… I am taking from them without giving back." The two texts never meet.

**Guardrail** shifts revealingly across three consecutive parts: a threshold routed around by reframing (18), an organisational switch deliberately disabled (19), then the cause of its own counter-argument (21), where hardening a classifier blocks a legitimate defender.

**Agentic direction** records a doubt. In February, part 03 asserts that "something essential carries through." In April, part 10 writes "perhaps it really does dispossess… I don't know." It is the only instance of a position abandoned mid-course, and it is flagged nowhere.

**Post-documentary** follows a less flattering trajectory: a poetic notion, then a search keyword in a visibility strategy document, then the object of explicit refusal in part 06, where it is replaced by *traboulage*.

**Operative image** is the corpus's most productive borrowing, and the only one whose every extension produces new thought: Farocki's concept applied to the benchmark (02), then to an editing function call (10), before being turned around into a question — what does Claude Code see when it looks at its user (08).

**Latent** moves from territory to explore (05) to raw material for editing (03), then to an architectural variable substantivised as "in the latent" (12), then to *limbo* (13).

## The absences

A glossary also states what is not there, and the survey found more than expected.

Several notions appearing in method descriptions have **no occurrence at all** in the written corpus: *cinématière liquide*, *movable blocks*, *desktop cinema*, *hypnagogic writing*. The title of part 02, *the sovereignty machine*, is never defined anywhere. *Traboulage*, coined and defined in part 06, is never reused — a hapax, whose etymology is not given and should not be reconstructed on the author's behalf.

The most significant absence is theoretical. **Bernard Stiegler**, presented elsewhere as a central reading frame, is reduced in this corpus to the single word *pharmakon*, attributed to its "Greek sense." Neither tertiary retention, nor proletarianisation, nor individuation, nor grammatisation appears. **Deleuze** is reduced to one sentence about the time-image, an inaccurate and unsourced paraphrase deployed in service of an anti-dispossession argument, plus a mention of the society of control in a bibliography only. **Adam Curtis** is credited as a co-author-persona and announced in part 10's summary — "Curtis on the system that captures" — but the part does not deliver: no concept is taken from him.

Finally, three expected technical terms are wholly absent: *tokenizer* never appears, tokens being discussed only as price and throughput; *edge computing* is systematically written in French as "calcul en périphérie"; and *cluster*, in the data-centre sense, is never used in its own right.

## What a glossary of one's own corpus is for

Not to fix a vocabulary, and certainly not to defend it. It serves three precise purposes, none of them decorative.

It makes displacements decidable. A term that changes meaning between two texts is not a problem while the shift is owned; it becomes one the moment it is invisible. Knowing that *tap* means two opposite things a month apart forces either a decision or a naming of the fork.

It exposes the gap between the claimed frame and the operative one. Invoking Stiegler as a reading grid and retaining one word from him is not a fault — it is information about the actual work, which only an exhaustive survey could produce.

It protects against oneself. A growing corpus spontaneously produces concepts that exist only in their titles, borrowings that serve only to authorise, and central notions used exactly once. The only known remedy is inventory, and inventory is useful only when taken without indulgence.

The survey behind this glossary was conducted by exhaustive file search, with occurrences verified one by one. Quotations whose source could not be recovered were discarded rather than reproduced, and search false positives — *Curtis* for Curtis Yarvin, *Benjamin* for Noys or Teitelbaum, *Ellul* inside "cellule," *aura* as the French future of "to have" — are flagged in the relevant files so they are not reintroduced at the next compilation.
